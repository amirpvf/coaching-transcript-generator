What this tool does — and doesn’t

The tool takes the audio of a coaching session and gives you a Word file in the mentor’s exact table template: four columns — time, speaker, text and competency. The last column stays empty for the mentor.

Done automatically

  • Full, word-for-word speech-to-text, with nothing summarised
  • Two-speaker separation, deciding who is coach and who is client
  • The start time of every turn
  • Persian typography fixes: zero-width non-joiners after «می» and «نمی», tanvin on words such as «مثلاً», no space before punctuation, cut-off words shown as «...»
  • Words the engine was unsure about are highlighted in yellow

Not done

  • No ICF competency analysis — that is the mentor’s job
  • No meaning-based correction of misheard words — the final review is yours
  • Nothing is saved: closing the page clears the transcript, so always download the Word file
What you need
  • Device: phone, tablet or computer. For sessions over an hour, a computer or iPad is safer because of memory.
  • Browser: Safari or Chrome. If you opened the link inside Telegram, use its menu to open it in your browser; Telegram’s built-in browser may fail to download the Word file or keep your key.
  • VPN: required if you are in Iran — see the VPN section.
  • ElevenLabs account and key: free; the illustrated steps are in box 1.
  • The recording: common formats such as m4a, mp3, wav, ogg and even video (mp4).
ElevenLabs free allowance and costs

Each free ElevenLabs account includes 4 hours 30 minutes of speech-to-text a month — roughly four one-hour sessions.

Worth knowing

  • Usage is counted by audio length, not file size. A 52-minute session uses 52 minutes.
  • The allowance renews monthly; your account page shows the date.
  • If a file is fully uploaded but the connection drops before the result comes back, it still counts. That is why long files are sent in parts — a drop only wastes one short part.
  • Checking what’s left: on the ElevenLabs site, tap your account name at the bottom of the left menu.
  • Test with a short file. A two- or three-minute clip is enough to try the tool.
  • Don’t create several free accounts. It breaks ElevenLabs’ rules and its abuse detection may block them.

If the free allowance isn’t enough

Paid plans include more: for example Starter has 27 hours a month and Creator 100 hours, with extra hours at about $0.22 each. Paid plans are also free of the free-tier VPN restriction. See the ElevenLabs site for current prices.

VPN: when it’s needed and why
  • Opening this page: no VPN needed — it is hosted on GitHub, which is reachable from Iran.
  • Creating your ElevenLabs account and key: VPN needed.
  • Pressing “Create transcript”: VPN needed, because the audio goes straight to ElevenLabs, which blocks Iranian IP addresses. Turn it on before pressing the button and keep it on until the result appears.
  • Downloading the Word file: no internet needed; it is built on your device.

One restriction to know about

On free accounts, ElevenLabs sometimes flags busy VPN IP addresses as unusual and blocks free use for that connection; you’ll see a specific message. Try another VPN server. If it keeps happening, only a paid plan avoids it.

Use a stable VPN — repeated disconnects are the most common reason a result gets lost.

Recording tips for the best result
  • Record in a quiet room; TV, air conditioning or street noise lowers accuracy.
  • In person, place the phone between you, close to both speakers.
  • Online, use the call app’s own recording if possible; it beats recording a phone speaker.
  • Avoid talking over each other; overlapping speech hurts both accuracy and speaker separation.
  • Start by asking the client’s consent to record — it is an ethical requirement and also helps the tool identify the coach, who usually asks more questions.
  • Say names clearly; proper names are the most often misheard words.

Why long files are sent in parts

A one-hour session is usually 50–100 MB. Sending it in one go over a VPN takes minutes, and a single blip loses the whole job — sometimes after the allowance has already been used.

So files over 12 MB are sent like this:

  1. The file is opened on your own device and split into parts of about 8 minutes.
  2. Cuts are placed in moments of silence so no word is cut in half.
  3. Parts are sent one at a time — you’ll see “Part 2 of 7”.
  4. If the connection drops, only that part is resent automatically, up to three times.
  5. The parts are then joined with correct times, and coach and client are identified within each part.

The result is the same as sending the whole file, and it uses the same allowance, which is counted by audio length.

Note: opening and splitting a very long file needs a lot of memory. If an older phone shows a file-opening error, try a computer or iPad.

Reviewing before you send it to your mentor
  • Yellow words: check each against the audio; its time in the recording is shown next to it.
  • Names: check them; proper names are the most error-prone.
  • Pauses and fillers (such as «اِ» and «امم») are kept on purpose, because the transcript must be complete and word-for-word.
  • English words spoken in the session stay in English letters.
  • Editing: it is an ordinary Word file — make any changes directly. Leave the competency column empty for the mentor.

Frequently asked questions

Can several people use it at once?

Yes, any number. There is no server; it runs separately on each person’s device. Each person should use their own key.

Can I share my key with a classmate?

Not recommended: your allowance gets split, and the key is like a password.

Does it work for English sessions?

Not yet — it is set up for Persian sessions and the mentor’s Persian Word template.

If I close the page, is the transcript lost?

Yes. It only exists while the page is open; always download the Word file.

Do I need Claude or another paid AI subscription?

No. Only an ElevenLabs account is needed; all processing and the Word file happen inside the page.

How accurate is it?

Very accurate in our tests on Persian sessions, but no tool is perfect — take the yellow-word review seriously.

Privacy: the technical details, for anyone who wants to be sure

There is no server

This tool is just a few static files that GitHub Pages delivers to your browser. There is no server, database or user account behind it. All the work happens inside your own browser.

Where exactly does the audio go?

Only, and directly, from your browser to api.elevenlabs.io, using your own key. Neither the maker of this page nor GitHub sees it.

Where is the Word file made?

In your browser’s memory, then downloaded straight to your device. No copy is uploaded or stored anywhere. Closing the page clears the transcript from memory.

Where is the key kept?

In this browser’s local storage on your device. “Remove key” deletes it completely.

A browser-enforced lock

The page carries a Content Security Policy that tells your browser it may not connect to any address except api.elevenlabs.io. Even if someone later added code to send data elsewhere, the browser would block it unless that policy were openly changed too.

No trackers

No analytics, cookies, ads, or third-party fonts and libraries; the font and the Word-building library are served alongside the page itself.

Check it yourself

  • The full code is readable in the public GitHub repository.
  • On a computer, open Developer Tools → Network and make a transcript: the only outside request is to api.elevenlabs.io.

One honest caveat

ElevenLabs itself receives the audio in order to transcribe it, under its own privacy policy. Mention in your client’s consent that the recording is processed by an AI transcription service.
